Base day — built to hit the moderate-week targets
Breakfast — 3 whole eggs soft-scrambled, 3 slices seeded sourdough, 1/2 avocado, 1 cup berries. 33P 111C 34F
Post-workout — the smoothie already defined in this app. 40P 42C 7F
Lunch — 4 oz chicken thigh, 2.5 cups cooked rice, 1.5 cups roasted vegetables, 1/2 tbsp olive oil. 43P 130C 16F
Dinner — 5 oz salmon, 300 g roasted sweet potato, large salad with 1 tbsp vinaigrette. 38P 70C 19F
Evening — 1 banana, 25 g almonds. 6P 32C 13F
Day total: 160P / 385C / 89F = 2,981 kcal — within 33 kcal and 2 g protein of the moderate-week target, which is well inside the error of any food estimate you will make.
Move between load levels with carbohydrate only. Protein and fat stay fixed all twelve weeks; carbohydrate is the only dial.
Deload week: −32 g carbs — drop one slice of sourdough and half a cup of rice.
High week: +12 g carbs — half a cup more rice.
Peak week: +25 g carbs — one cup more rice, or a second banana.
Swaps that keep the macros. 5 oz salmon ↔ 6 oz cod plus 1 tsp oil ↔ 5 oz 90/10 beef. 4 oz chicken thigh ↔ 5 oz chicken breast plus 1 tsp oil. Rice ↔ potato, pasta, or quinoa at equal cooked carbohydrate. Sourdough ↔ oats at 1 slice per 20 g dry.
Two specifics for you. The BPN vanilla whey is a concentrate and casein blend and has been giving you GI symptoms — that is the lactose, and at four scoops-worth of protein a week during a surplus it is worth solving rather than tolerating. Switch the post-workout smoothie to a whey isolate or a non-dairy protein; the macros above are unchanged either way. And at 5,280 ft: hard-boiled eggs need about 3 extra minutes, rice and quinoa need more water and longer, and roasted vegetables run slightly shorter than the recipe says.
Sunday, about 50 minutes, covers lunches. Oven to 425°F. Chicken thighs and two trays of vegetables in together. Rice cooker on. Sweet potatoes on the lower rack while the oven is already hot. Portion the chicken and rice into five containers; keep vegetables in one large box and add them cold at assembly so they do not go soft. Salmon is cooked fresh at dinner — it is the one thing that does not survive batching well.
This is a template to eat against, not a prescription to obey. Hit protein first, then total calories; the distribution across meals has no established effect on hypertrophy at a fixed daily total.
